## [Academy Transaction Note: Capturing Objects in Microgravity Using Induction]

**[S. R. Greaves]**, [C. R. Lambert, K. Dhekar, J. J. Abbott].  
*[Acta Astronautica]* · [2026] · **[Under revision for resubmission]**

[We discuss a technology concept that uses magnetic induction from three or more field sources to pull disparate conductive objects in microgravity into a collection device, without precise information about the number, location, or composition of the objects. We use 2D and 3D numerical simulations, real-life water-based planar experiments and real-life force and torque characterization to explore the potential of this concept.]

## [Interpolating Models of Magnetically Induced Wrenches in SO(3)]

**[S. R. Greaves]**, [J. J. Abbott].  
*[IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters]* · [2027] · **[In preperation]**

[We propose a novel method of interpolating between a sparse data set on SO(3), by treating the encapsulating simplex on the surface of the hypersphere as a set of basis vectors, and decomposing weights to create a linear combination of known induced wrenches.]

## [Force and Torque Induced on a Conductive Nonmagnetic Cylinder by a Rotating Magnetic Dipole.]

[L. M. Barker, T. J. Allen], **S. R. Greaves**, [J. J. Abbott].  
*[IEEE Transaction on Magnetics]* · [2026] · **[Under review]**

[In this paper, we describe the nondimensional empirical modeling of the force and torque that is induced on a cylinder which is electrically conductive, but not ferromagnetic, when it is placed in a rotating magnetic dipole field. The force and torque are a result of eddy currents being induced by, and then interacting with, the rotating magnetic field. We consider solid cylinders as well as cylinders of arbitrary wall thickness, and we consider cylinders with length-to-diameter aspect ratios of 0.5, 1.0, and 2.0. Other independent variables include the strength and angular velocity of the magnetic dipole, the conductivity and size of the cylinder, and the distance and pose of the cylinder with respect to the magnetic dipole. Our model is a far-field model, meaning that it assumes some minimal distance between the cylinder and the magnetic dipole. This work is motivated by on-orbit servicing and remediation of space debris, which contain large fractions of aluminum, and for which the thin-walled cylinder is arguably the most important geometry. Our model can be used for mission-specific feasibility studies, as well as to accurately initialize adaptive induction-based manipulation controllers.]

## [First Demonstration of Six-Degree-of-Freedom Manipulation Using Magnetic Induction Enabled by a Novel Neutral-Buoyancy Simulation of Microgravity. IEEE Transactions on Robotics]

[T. J. Allen, K. A. Ben-Jacob, R. R. Scoggan, T. C. Wilcox], **S. R. Greaves**, [E. M. VanderHoeven, A. P. Newey, L. E. Martin, D. A. Wall, G. F. Tabor, J. J. Abbott]. 
*[IEEE Transactions on Robotics]* · [2026] · **[In preperation]**

[There is a growing interest in the use of robotics to perform on-orbit servicing to refuel, repair, or upgrade space assets, and to capture and remove derelict objects from orbit. Many of these resident space objects were not engineered to be manipulated, many are fragile, and many are tumbling in an uncontrolled way. As a result, researchers are pursuing noncontact manipulation techniques such as magnetic induction, which involves using a dynamic magnetic field to induce eddy currents in an electrically conductive nonmagnetic object, which in turn induces force and/or torque as the eddy currents interact with the applied magnetic field. It has been shown that six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) manipulation is theoretically achievable using magnetic induction by surrounding the object by two or more controllable dipole field sources. However, to date, all physical manipulation experiments have been 3-DOF planar experiments, and 6-DOF manipulation has been limited to purely numerical simulations and hardware-in-the-loop simulations. In this paper, we describe a neutral-buoyancy simulation of magnetic manipulation in microgravity. Our design is based on a dodecahedron shell with each of its 12 faces equipped with a fiducial marker for localization based on machine vision, which is compatible with magnetic actuation. The object of interest fits within a spherical chamber at the center of the shell. Using this novel neutral-buoyancy simulation of microgravity, we demonstrate physical 6-DOF manipulation using magnetic induction for the first time. Although motivated by magnetic induction, our neutral buoyancy simulation can also be applied to ferromagnetism-based manipulation.]

# About me

I'm Sawyer Greaves, a student in the University of Utah's combined B.S./M.S. program in mechanical engineering and robotics. I work with Prof. Jake J. Abbott in the [Magnetic & Medical Robotics Lab](https://mmrobotics.mech.utah.edu/), where my research focuses on modeling and simulating magnetic manipulation of conductive objects in microgravity.

I'm applying to PhD programs in robotics. My interests include dynamics and control, perception, simulation, and manipulation. My long-term goal is to develop robots that can explore difficult planetary terrain, where reliable motion depends on understanding both the robot and its surroundings.

## Education

- **M.S. in Robotics**, University of Utah — expected May 2027.
- **B.S. in Mechanical Engineering**, University of Utah — expected December 2026.

## Research and engineering

Since joining the lab in May 2025, I have developed the rigid-body dynamics component of a hardware-in-the-loop simulator, conducted experiments on interactions between nearby conductive objects, and proposed an interpolation method for orientation-dependent magnetic force and torque models. Read more about my [research](./research/) and [research manuscripts](./publications.md).

I also enjoy building complete systems. My [engineering projects](./projects/) include a camera-tracking mechanism, an autonomous block-sorting robot, and an industry-sponsored manufacturing machine. Before joining the lab, I worked as a research engineering intern at Sublime Laser, developing laser-machining processes and operating and maintaining equipment used to manufacture medical devices. That experience taught me to pay close attention to repeatability, inspection, and reliability.

## Teaching and mentoring

An after-hours Python Bootcamp was an important starting point for my interest in programming. I later returned as an instructor and mentored approximately 40 students over two semesters, helping them build practical Python skills for engineering.

As a teaching assistant for junior-level mechatronics, I lead labs, help students troubleshoot their systems, and advise six robotics project teams each week on mechanical, electrical, and software design. I value helping students connect what they already know to the unfamiliar parts of a project, much as my own mentors have done for me.

I have also served on the Undergraduate Student Advisory Council, contributing student input on curriculum and faculty-related decisions.
